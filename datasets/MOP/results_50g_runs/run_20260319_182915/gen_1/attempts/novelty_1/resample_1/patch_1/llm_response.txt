An elegant and highly effective way to solve this multi-objective optimization problem is to use a physics-inspired gradient descent approach. 

### Method Explanation

The problem requires us to minimize the product of three objectives:
1. **F1 (Flying Distance)**: Acts as a tension force, pulling the path into a straight line.
2. **F2 (School Noise Proximity)**: Acts as a repulsive force, pushing the path away from the school.
3. **F3 (Base Station Signal Drop)**: Acts as an attractive force, pulling the path towards the base station.

Since the final score is the product of these three metrics ($F_1 \times F_2 \times F_3$), minimizing the product is mathematically equivalent to minimizing the sum of their logarithms ($\log F_1 + \log F_2 + \log F_3$). 
The gradient of this logarithmic objective with respect to any Y-coordinate is simply the sum of the relative gradients:
$$ \nabla_{y_i} \text{Objective} = \frac{\nabla_{y_i} F_1}{F_1} + \frac{\nabla_{y_i} F_2}{F_2} + \frac{\nabla_{y_i} F_3}{F_3} $$

This is a beautiful property because it automatically balances the scales of the three completely different metrics without needing to know their exact weighting constants! 

We can implement a lightweight **Adam Optimizer** from scratch in pure Python. We initialize the path as a straight line and iteratively update the Y-coordinates. Adam is perfect here because it normalizes the gradients, preventing numerical instability if the drone gets too close to the school (which would otherwise cause a massive gradient spike), and ensures smooth convergence within the 12-second timeout.
