# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns
- **The current best result is still the Gaussian Control-Point Drone Path, and the new comparisons strengthen the case for its design choices.** Its **12.82** score with **F1=1.52, F2=0.25, F3=33.55** remains better than all newly summarized programs, including **Adam-Optimized Energy Drone Path** (**13.35**), **Hybrid Heuristic Adam Drone Path** (**14.53**), **Adaptive Contour Drone Path** (**24.29**), **Gaussian Basis Drone Path Search** (**26.17**), and **Base-Targeted Sine Gaussian Path** (**53.85**). The standout pattern is a **low-dimensional Gaussian control-point parameterization optimized directly on the multiplicative proxy**, which continues to outperform denser additive-energy optimizers.

- **Direct alignment with the actual ranking metric remains the most successful algorithmic change observed.** The best program explicitly minimizes **`F1 * F2 * F3`** through `proxy_score`, while the newer Adam/active-contour programs optimize additive combinations of length, smoothness, school repulsion, and base attraction. Even when those programs achieved favorable individual metrics—e.g. **Adam-Optimized Energy Drone Path** reached **F1=1.46** and **F3=30.98**—they still trailed the control-point method because their search objective was less tightly coupled to the evaluated product.

- **Heuristic multi-start initialization around both school and base continues to correlate with strong scores.** The best program’s discrete grid over school-biased and base-biased control offsets appears more effective than starting from a single straight-line or single-shape initialization. This pattern is reinforced by the fact that the best non-control-point entrants with heuristic + refinement pipelines—**Adam-Optimized Energy Drone Path** (**13.35**) and **Hybrid Heuristic Adam Drone Path** (**14.53**)—were competitive, while more one-shot analytic shaping such as **Base-Targeted Sine Gaussian Path** collapsed to **53.85**.

- **Aggressive school avoidance that stays geometrically smooth is still a major contributor to strong total score.** The best program’s **F2=0.25** remains the strongest balance among the top performers. Newer competitors like **Hybrid Heuristic Adam Drone Path** (**F2=0.35**) and **Adaptive Contour Drone Path** (**F2=0.37**) confirmed that good school avoidance helps, but they could not match the control-point method’s total score because they either inflated other terms or failed to preserve the same overall product balance.

## Ineffective Approaches
- **Base-focused shaping without sufficiently strong score-driven refinement remains weak, especially on F3.** The clearest example is **Base-Targeted Sine Gaussian Path**, which used an analytic warp centered on the base but still scored a poor **53.85**, with **F3=33.74** and a much worse **F2=1.28**. This shows that merely adding a base-oriented deformation field does not reliably improve the full product.

- **Additive energy minimization continues to underperform direct product minimization, even when it produces good-looking component values.** **Adam-Optimized Energy Drone Path** scored **13.35** and **Hybrid Heuristic Adam Drone Path** scored **14.53**, both with relatively good **F1/F2** values, but both were explicitly flagged for persistent signal-loss tradeoff issues. Their implementations are more sophisticated numerically than the current best, yet they still underperform because their objective structure is less aligned with evaluation.

- **Separately normalized or heavily engineered contour-force updates remain vulnerable to weak base/signal behavior.** **Adaptive Contour Drone Path** combined normalized gradients, adaptive weights, active-contour refinement, and periodic smoothing, but scored only **24.29** with a very poor **F3=38.16**. This extends the previous pattern that complex force balancing can become misaligned with the true score.

- **Broader surrogate searches still lag when the surrogate does not match the actual metric closely enough.** **Gaussian Basis Drone Path Search** used coarse-to-fine grid search plus a surrogate objective over path length inflation, school penalty, base distance, and smoothness, yet only reached **26.17**. That is far behind the current best despite using a similarly structured Gaussian basis, indicating that **parameterization alone is not enough without direct score alignment**.

## Implementation Insights
- **What makes the current best program effective is the combination of a smooth basis representation and exact proxy-score optimization.** In code, path deformation is represented by **12 Gaussian control amplitudes** over a linear baseline, and `proxy_score` computes the same three-way structure that governs ranking: normalized path length, school exposure, and cumulative base distance. This avoids the mismatch seen in programs like **Adam-Optimized Energy Drone Path** and **Hybrid Heuristic Adam Drone Path**, which optimize additive handcrafted energies instead.

- **The current best gets useful expressiveness from control points without paying the instability cost of full per-point optimization.** Compared with dense intermediate-y optimizers such as **Adam-Optimized Energy Drone Path** and **Adaptive Contour Drone Path**, the control-point model produces globally smooth deformations automatically through Gaussian overlap. That likely explains why it can maintain a strong **F2=0.25** without requiring explicit Laplacian, curvature, fourth-order, or repeated smoothing machinery.

- **Discrete multi-start over school/base-biased initial shapes is a concrete implementation advantage in the best result.** The nested `scale_s` and `scale_b` loops try structured initial control settings before local search. This is more effective than the single deterministic shaping used by **Base-Targeted Sine Gaussian Path** and more basin-aware than straight-line-only refinement approaches. The continued gap over the Adam-based methods suggests initialization quality materially affected final optimization.

- **Simple coordinate descent was empirically stronger than heavier optimizers when paired with the right representation.** The current best uses **25 rounds** of coordinate-wise trial moves with shrinking step size, yet still beat **400-iteration Adam** in **Adam-Optimized Energy Drone Path** and **260-iteration Adam** in **Hybrid Heuristic Adam Drone Path**. The evidence across these runs continues to show that **objective fidelity and parameterization mattered more than optimizer sophistication**.

## Performance Analysis
- **The current best remains clearly ahead of the newly evaluated field.** Relative to **Gaussian Control-Point Drone Path** at **12.82**, the strongest new entrant, **Adam-Optimized Energy Drone Path**, is about **4.1% worse** at **13.35**. **Hybrid Heuristic Adam Drone Path** is about **13.3% worse** at **14.53**. The remaining new methods trail far more severely: **Adaptive Contour Drone Path** (**24.29**), **Gaussian Basis Drone Path Search** (**26.17**), and **Base-Targeted Sine Gaussian Path** (**53.85**).

- **The new results reinforce that top-tier performance comes from balancing the full product, not from winning F1 or F3 individually.** The current best does **not** have the best **F1** or **F3** among these programs: **Adam-Optimized Energy Drone Path** has better **F1=1.46** and **F3=30.98**, and **Hybrid Heuristic Adam Drone Path** has **F3=29.99**. But the best program’s much lower **F2=0.25** is enough to give it the best overall multiplier score.

- **F3 remains the dominant failure mode for many weaker programs, but the new comparisons show that F3 alone does not determine the winner.** Very weak methods like **Adaptive Contour Drone Path** (**F3=38.16**) and **Base-Targeted Sine Gaussian Path** (**F3=33.74** with poor **F2**) confirm that high signal-loss often drives bad totals. However, the current best still wins despite a worse **F3=33.55** than some Adam-based competitors, because its school-noise term is substantially better.

- **A clearer hierarchy is emerging across implementation styles.** Best is still the **direct product-optimized Gaussian control-point model** (**12.82**). Next are the **heuristic-plus-Adam dense path optimizers** around **13.35–14.53**. Below that sit **surrogate-searched Gaussian-basis and active-contour variants** around **24–26**, and far below them are **analytic one-shot warping methods** like **Base-Targeted Sine Gaussian Path** at **53.85**. This strengthens the earlier conclusion that the strongest observed programs combine **moderate-dimensional smooth geometry, explicit score alignment, and structured initialization**.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Extend the 12-control Gaussian basis with a second, wider deformation scale while keeping the same `proxy_score` objective.** Concretely, keep the current 12 narrow Gaussian amplitudes and add either 4–6 wide Gaussians or one low-frequency global bump basis spanning the whole route. This preserves the winning smooth low-dimensional representation, but gives the optimizer more freedom to improve `F3` through broad base-oriented bends without sacrificing the excellent school-avoidance behavior that currently drives `F2`.

2. **Broaden the heuristic multi-start library beyond the current `scale_s × scale_b` grid using structured asymmetric seeds.** Add initializations such as left-heavy and right-heavy school repulsion, one or two bridge bumps between school and base, and seeds that place a single strong deviation just before or after the school’s x-position. The best program already benefits from basin-aware initialization, and the recent comparisons suggest that better starting shapes are more valuable than switching to a more sophisticated optimizer.

3. **Augment coordinate descent with pairwise or small-block updates for neighboring controls, still evaluated by exact product score.** For example, after each single-control sweep, try joint updates on `(i, i+1)` using a tiny grid like `{−h, 0, +h}²`, or allow one control to rise while its neighbor falls. This directly targets a likely limitation of the current method: some useful `F2/F3` tradeoffs require coordinated local shape changes that single-coordinate moves may miss.

4. **Allow limited optimization of Gaussian center x-positions, with monotonic ordering and spacing regularization.** Start from the current evenly spaced `ctrl_xs`, but let each center shift within a small window while enforcing sorted order and penalizing large spacing irregularities. This is a natural extension of the current best design and could concentrate flexibility near the school or base where it matters most, instead of spending equal representational capacity everywhere.

5. **Make the search hierarchical: solve a coarse control problem first, then refine in the current 12-control space.** A practical mutation is to optimize 4–6 coarse Gaussian amplitudes with the same initialization and proxy score, interpolate that solution into 12 controls, and then run the existing local descent. This follows the strongest observed pattern—compact smooth parameterization with exact score alignment—but may find better large-scale route geometry before fine local adjustments lock in the final `F2/F3` balance.
