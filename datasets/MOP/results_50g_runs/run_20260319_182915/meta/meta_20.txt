# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns
- **The new current best result comes from a compact but directly score-aligned control-point model optimized on the actual proxy product \(F1 \times F2 \times F3\).** The **Gaussian Control-Point Drone Path** is now the strongest observed program at **12.82** with **F1=1.52, F2=0.25, F3=33.55**, outperforming the previous best active-contour family. Its key implementation change is the combination of **12 evenly spaced Gaussian control points**, **heuristic school/base-biased initialization**, and **coordinate descent on the full multiplicative proxy**, which clearly improved overall balance relative to the earlier pointwise smoothness-force methods.
- **Low-dimensional, flexible deformation can beat dense per-point optimization when the search is tightly coupled to the true tradeoff metric.** Earlier global insights favored direct active-contour updates, but this new result shows that a **moderate-dimensional basis with explicit proxy-score minimization** can do better than local gradient methods. Compared with **Physics-Based Gradient Drone Path** (**14.50**) and **Adam-Optimized Active Contour Path** (**14.01**), the control-point method achieved a meaningfully lower total score despite a somewhat worse **F3**, because it simultaneously drove **F2 down to 0.25**, the best among the top contenders.
- **Heuristic initialization around both school and base remains a successful pattern when followed by local refinement.** The current best program does not rely on a single straight-line start; it tests a grid of school-biased and base-biased control offsets before descent. That appears materially better than methods that start only from a straight line and optimize from there, such as **Adam-Optimized Active Contour Path** (**14.01**) and **Physics-Based Gradient Drone Path** (**14.50**), both of which remained trapped in a higher-**F3** regime around **30.1** and a higher overall score.
- **Strong school avoidance continues to be one of the clearest contributors to competitive total score, provided distance inflation stays moderate.** The current best’s **F2=0.2512** is better than **Physics-Based Gradient Drone Path** (**0.32**), **Adam-Optimized Active Contour Path** (**0.32**), and **Heuristic Smoothed Multi-Objective Path** (**0.36**). This confirms the earlier pattern that aggressive but controlled school repulsion is valuable; the new evidence adds that it can be achieved through **Gaussian basis placement and offset search**, not only through per-point repulsion gradients.

## Ineffective Approaches
- **Improving only school avoidance without preserving the full global product tradeoff still hurts total performance.** The **Multi-Phase Adam Drone Path** reached the lowest **F2** among these summaries at **0.21**, but its **F1=1.92** and especially **F3=40.39** pushed the score up to **16.37**, far worse than the current best **12.82**. This matches prior evidence that over-prioritizing one objective in staged schedules can degrade the product score.
- **Fixed-x active-contour variants remain limited when signal-loss optimization is not strong enough.** Both **Physics-Based Gradient Drone Path** (**14.50**, **F3=30.08**) and **Adam-Optimized Active Contour Path** (**14.01**, **F3=30.14**) are solid but were explicitly flagged for weak signal-loss behavior. Even though their **F3** values are numerically lower than the current best’s **33.55**, they did not achieve as favorable a full multiplier balance as the control-point method, reinforcing that fixed-x local updates alone do not guarantee the best overall product.
- **Weight-scheduled or separately normalized multi-objective gradients can become misaligned with the true evaluation objective.** The **Multi-Phase Adam Drone Path** used stage-wise emphasis and objective-normalized gradients but scored **16.37**, while the **Heuristic Smoothed Multi-Objective Path** used separately normalized gradient terms and scored an even worse **21.70**. In both cases, evaluator feedback specifically pointed to persistent weakness in **F3**, suggesting these balancing schemes did not translate into the actual optimum of the evaluated score.
- **Analytic one-shot bending plus smoothing remains weaker than direct parameter search on score.** The **Heuristic Smoothed Multi-Objective Path** produced a valid smooth path but landed at **21.70** with **F2=0.36** and **F3=36.95**, substantially behind the control-point coordinate-descent approach. This continues the broader pattern that handcrafted deformation fields without strong score-driven refinement leave too much performance on the table.

## Implementation Insights
- **What makes the current best program effective is its explicit optimization of the same multiplicative structure that determines ranking.** In code, `proxy_score` computes **normalized path length**, **school inverse-distance exposure**, and **base distance loss**, then multiplies them directly. That is a sharper alignment with evaluation than the active-contour programs, which optimize additive surrogate energies and only indirectly influence the final product.
- **The current best gains useful flexibility without the instability of full per-point optimization.** Its path is represented by **12 Gaussian control-point amplitudes** over a linear baseline, with `sigma` tied to control spacing. This produces smooth, globally coordinated deformations automatically, avoiding the need for explicit curvature penalties, Laplacians, or frequent smoothing passes used in the gradient-based programs.
- **Discrete multi-start initialization is a meaningful implementation advantage in this result.** The nested search over `scale_s` and `scale_b` creates candidate shapes biased near the school and base before refinement. That is a concrete difference from **Physics-Based Gradient Drone Path** and **Adam-Optimized Active Contour Path**, which both begin from a straight line only. The score gap suggests that better initialization helped the control-point search find a stronger basin.
- **Simple coordinate descent was more effective here than heavier optimizers when paired with a low-dimensional parameterization.** The current best uses only **25 outer rounds** with four trial step sizes per coordinate and a shrinking `step_size`. Despite its simplicity, this beat **600-step Adam** (**14.01**) and **800-step gradient descent with Gauss-Seidel updates** (**14.50**), indicating that optimizer sophistication mattered less than parameterization and direct score alignment in these evaluated runs.

## Performance Analysis
- **The current best program establishes a new leading score of 12.82, surpassing the previously best-known active-contour result of 14.19 by about 9.7%.** It also beats the newly summarized **Adam-Optimized Active Contour Path** (**14.01**) by about **8.5%** and **Physics-Based Gradient Drone Path** (**14.50**) by about **11.6%**. This is the most important updated trend: the leaderboard is no longer led by direct per-point active contours, but by a Gaussian control-point model tuned against the product score.
- **Among competitive programs, the winning profile is still balanced rather than extreme on any single metric.** The current best does not have the lowest **F1** or **F3**; **Adam-Optimized Active Contour Path** has slightly lower **F1=1.46** and **F3=30.14**, and **Physics-Based Gradient Drone Path** has **F3=30.08**. But the current best’s much better **F2=0.25** appears to compensate enough to produce the lowest multiplier score overall.
- **F3 remains important, but the new data shows it is not the only separator at the top end.** Previous insights emphasized **F3** as the dominant differentiator, and that remains true for weaker methods like **Multi-Phase Adam Drone Path** (**F3=40.39**, score **16.37**) and **Heuristic Smoothed Multi-Objective Path** (**F3=36.95**, score **21.70**). However, the current best demonstrates that a sufficiently strong reduction in **F2** can outweigh having a somewhat worse **F3** than the active-contour baselines.
- **The newest results split into a clear performance hierarchy by optimization style.** Best is the **Gaussian Control-Point Drone Path** at **12.82**; next are the **direct active-contour optimizers** around **14.0–14.5**; then the **staged/normalized or heuristic smoothing methods** at **16.37** and **21.70**. This suggests that, in actual evaluated outcomes, the strongest methods are those that combine **smooth but flexible geometry**, **direct score-aware search**, and **enough expressiveness to shape school/base tradeoffs without overfitting local updates**.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Upgrade the control-point basis from single-scale Gaussians to a small multi-scale basis, then keep the same direct proxy-score search.** For each of the 12 control locations, add either a second wider Gaussian or a low-frequency global bump basis so the path can make broad base-oriented bends while still preserving sharp local school avoidance. This directly extends the current winner’s low-dimensional, smooth parameterization and is promising because the main remaining weakness is likely a constrained `F3` tradeoff under the current single-width basis.

2. **Expand the initialization stage into a richer multi-start library centered on school/base geometry, not just scalar offset grids.** In addition to the current `scale_s × scale_b` search, try candidates such as: school-side repulsion only, base-side attraction only, asymmetric left/right bumps, and one or two “bridge” bumps placed between school and base. The current best already benefits from heuristic multi-starts, so a broader but still cheap set of structured initial shapes is a high-probability way to land in a better basin before coordinate descent.

3. **Keep optimizing the exact multiplicative proxy, but switch from single-coordinate descent to block or pairwise coordinate updates for nearby control points.** Try updating adjacent control-point pairs jointly with a small discrete step grid, or alternate single-point sweeps with 2-point block sweeps. This is well aligned with the best program’s success from direct score minimization, and it may unlock coordinated deformations that reduce `F3` without immediately sacrificing the excellent `F2`.

4. **Introduce limited optimization of control-point x-positions while preserving order and endpoint anchoring.** Instead of fixing only amplitudes, allow each Gaussian center to move slightly within a bounded window, with a penalty to keep spacing regular and monotone. This targets a key limitation noted in earlier strong methods—fixed geometry can trap the `F2/F3` balance—and could let the path place flexibility more precisely near the school or base.

5. **Make the search explicitly hierarchical: first optimize a very low-dimensional coarse path, then refine with the full 12-control model.** For example, run the same proxy-score descent on 4–6 coarse control points, upsample that solution into 12 controls, and then do the current local refinement. This builds directly on the winning recipe of smooth compact parameterization, but may improve robustness and lower score by finding better large-scale structure before spending capacity on local adjustments.
