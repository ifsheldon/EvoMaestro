# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns
- **The current best program, Gaussian Control-Point Drone Path, is still the strongest confirmation that direct optimization of the true multiplicative objective is the most effective pattern observed.** Its score of **12.82** with **F1=1.52, F2=0.25, F3=33.55** remains better than all newly listed programs, including **Heuristic Seed with Adam Path Refinement** at **19.14**, **Gaussian Windowed Grid-Search Path** at **21.66**, and **Multi-Scale Gaussian Path Optimizer** at **14.57**. The key implementation choice is explicit minimization of the exact proxy structure `F1 * F2 * F3`, rather than an additive surrogate.

- **Low-dimensional smooth Gaussian parameterizations continue to outperform dense per-point y-optimization.** The best program uses only **12 Gaussian control amplitudes** over a linear baseline, while weaker methods such as **Multi-Seed Adam Path Optimizer** (**32.01**) and **Gaussian Warm-Start Adam Path Optimizer** (**51.43**) optimize many waypoint y-values directly with curvature/smoothing penalties. The smoother, lower-dimensional representation appears to preserve strong **F2** without requiring heavy regularization.

- **Structured multi-start initialization around school and base remains a strong recurring advantage.** The current best explicitly scans `scale_s` × `scale_b` over **25 school/base-biased initializations** before local refinement. This remains more effective than single analytic warm starts such as **Gaussian Warm-Start Adam Path Optimizer** (**51.43**) or the simpler discrete perturbation family in **Gaussian Windowed Grid-Search Path** (**21.66**). The evidence still supports basin coverage through a small but targeted initialization set.

- **Strong total scores come from preserving excellent school avoidance even without winning F3 outright.** The best program does not have the lowest F3 among the compared methods, yet its **F2=0.25** is substantially better than **Heuristic Seed with Adam Path Refinement** (**F2=0.50**), **Gaussian Windowed Grid-Search Path** (**F2=0.39**), and **Multi-Seed Adam Path Optimizer** (**F2=0.80**). This reinforces the earlier pattern that smooth, aggressive school avoidance is one of the clearest contributors to top product-score performance.

## Ineffective Approaches
- **Additive surrogate objectives with many handcrafted penalties continue to underperform exact product alignment.** **Multi-Seed Adam Path Optimizer** combines log path length, school repulsion, base distance, curvature, ripple suppression, and tether penalties, yet scores only **32.01**. **Gaussian Warm-Start Adam Path Optimizer** similarly uses a normalized multi-objective Adam loss with weight scheduling and reaches **51.43**. In both cases, evaluator feedback explicitly points to unresolved base/signal tradeoff issues.

- **Improving F1 and F2 alone without matching the multiplicative ranking metric does not reliably produce competitive total scores.** **Multi-Scale Gaussian Path Optimizer** achieves very strong **F2=0.2109**, even lower than the current best, but its score is still worse at **14.57** because **F1=1.8098** and **F3=38.17** are too high. Likewise, **Heuristic Seed with Adam Path Refinement** gets a respectable **19.14** with good distance and school terms, but its **F3=31.82** still dominates according to evaluator feedback.

- **Base-oriented shaping by itself remains insufficient when the search space or optimization procedure is too coarse.** **Gaussian Windowed Grid-Search Path** adds one school-repelling and one base-attracting Gaussian with a discrete search over amplitudes and widths, but still lands at **21.66** with **F3=39.84**, the worst F3 among the newly listed valid programs except the much weaker warm-start Adam case. This mirrors earlier evidence that simple base attraction fields do not automatically yield good signal-loss outcomes.

- **Heavy optimization machinery and long iteration counts have not translated into better scores when paired with weaker objective formulations.** **Gaussian Warm-Start Adam Path Optimizer** uses **720 Adam steps** and phase-based weight scheduling, yet performs dramatically worse than the current best’s simple coordinate descent. The same pattern appears in **Multi-Seed Adam Path Optimizer**, whose robustness and validity did not compensate for score misalignment.

## Implementation Insights
- **What makes the current best program effective is the tight coupling between representation and evaluation metric.** In code, it defines the path as a linear baseline plus **12 overlapping Gaussian control functions**, then evaluates candidates with a `proxy_score` that directly computes the same three components that matter for ranking: normalized length, cumulative school noise, and cumulative base distance. This is a cleaner and more faithful construction than the additive energies used by the Adam-based programs.

- **The current best gains smoothness implicitly from basis overlap instead of explicit regularizers.** Unlike **Multi-Seed Adam Path Optimizer**, which needs second-derivative curvature, fourth-derivative ripple suppression, and passive smoothing, or **Multi-Scale Gaussian Path Optimizer**, which adds second-difference penalties and periodic reprojection smoothing, the best program achieves a competitive smooth path with no separate curvature term. The Gaussian basis itself appears to provide enough regularity.

- **The best implementation uses very simple but effective local search mechanics.** After the structured school/base initialization sweep, it performs **25 rounds of coordinate descent** with a shrinking step size over the 12 control amplitudes. This remains notable because it still beats more numerically sophisticated optimizers such as the **180-step Adam** in **Heuristic Seed with Adam Path Refinement** and the **720-step Adam** in **Gaussian Warm-Start Adam Path Optimizer**.

- **The strongest competing non-best programs still support the value of smooth parametric path families, but only when objective mismatch is limited.** **Multi-Scale Gaussian Path Optimizer** at **14.57** is closer to the best than the dense-waypoint Adam methods, suggesting that Gaussian basis parameterization is indeed beneficial. However, because it optimizes weighted proxies rather than the exact product, it still trails the control-point method.

## Performance Analysis
- **The current best remains clearly ahead of the newly summarized field.** Against **Gaussian Control-Point Drone Path** at **12.82**, the closest newly listed competitor is **Multi-Scale Gaussian Path Optimizer** at **14.57**, about **13.6% worse**. **Heuristic Seed with Adam Path Refinement** at **19.14** is about **49.3% worse**, **Gaussian Windowed Grid-Search Path** at **21.66** is about **69.0% worse**, and **Gaussian Warm-Start Adam Path Optimizer** at **51.43** is about **301% worse**.

- **The score hierarchy still strongly favors direct product optimization over additive or loosely related surrogates.** The best exact-product control-point method scores **12.82**, while additive Adam variants span **19.14**, **32.01**, and **51.43**. Even when those methods produce decent-looking individual terms, evaluator comments repeatedly identify unresolved signal-loss tradeoffs, indicating that surrogate optimization continues to miss the actual ranking balance.

- **F3 remains a common weakness across many programs, but the results show that F3 alone does not determine the winner.** The best program’s **F3=33.55** is not exceptional: **Heuristic Seed with Adam Path Refinement** has a better **F3=31.82**. But that program still loses badly overall because its **F2=0.50** is much worse than the best program’s **0.25**. Conversely, **Multi-Scale Gaussian Path Optimizer** achieves excellent **F2=0.21** but loses on **F1** and **F3**. The observed ranking continues to reward balanced product behavior rather than domination of one term.

- **A stable implementation hierarchy is becoming more evident across all evaluated programs.** Best remains the **direct product-optimized Gaussian control-point model** (**12.82**). Next are **smooth parametric or heuristic-refined models** that are still somewhat misaligned with the metric, such as **Multi-Scale Gaussian Path Optimizer** (**14.57**) and **Heuristic Seed with Adam Path Refinement** (**19.14**). Below them are **coarser grid-search or dense contour/Adam methods** like **Gaussian Windowed Grid-Search Path** (**21.66**) and **Multi-Seed Adam Path Optimizer** (**32.01**), with **Gaussian Warm-Start Adam Path Optimizer** (**51.43**) showing that more refinement steps do not rescue a poorly aligned objective.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Add a second, broader Gaussian basis layer while keeping the exact `proxy_score = F1 * F2 * F3` objective unchanged.** Concretely, retain the current 12 control amplitudes and add 4–6 wide Gaussians with larger `sigma` values, optimized jointly by the same local search. This builds directly on the strongest observed pattern—smooth low-dimensional Gaussian parameterization—but gives the model extra capacity for large-scale bends that may reduce `F3` without destroying the excellent `F2`.

2. **Expand the initialization set with asymmetric and event-localized seeds instead of only the current `scale_s × scale_b` symmetric sweep.** Useful additions include: a single bump just before the school x-position, a single bump just after it, left-heavy school repulsion, right-heavy school repulsion, and one “bridge” bump between school and base. The current best already benefits from targeted basin coverage, and recent results suggest better seeds are more valuable than switching to heavier optimizers.

3. **Upgrade the coordinate descent to include small block moves on neighboring controls under the same exact product score.** After each single-coordinate sweep, try joint updates for pairs like `(i, i+1)` or `(i-1, i, i+1)` using a tiny candidate set such as `{-h, 0, +h}` per variable, accepting only score improvements. This is a natural extension of the current solver and directly addresses the likely failure mode where improving `F2` and `F3` requires coordinated local shape changes that one-at-a-time updates cannot reach.

4. **Introduce a hierarchical coarse-to-fine search over the same Gaussian path family.** First optimize a very small model, such as 4 or 6 Gaussian controls, then interpolate those amplitudes into the 12-control representation and run the existing refinement; optionally finish with the added wide-scale controls from recommendation 1. This preserves the successful exact-objective alignment while improving the chance of finding better global route geometry before fine-scale local descent commits to a basin.

5. **Allow limited adaptation of control center locations near important x-regions while preserving ordered, smooth structure.** Start from evenly spaced `ctrl_xs`, but permit each center to shift within a small window, especially around the school and base x-positions, with monotonic ordering enforced and only occasional center-update steps. The current best spends equal representational capacity everywhere; reallocating some flexibility toward the most decision-critical regions is a promising way to improve the `F2`/`F3` balance without moving to a dense waypoint parameterization.
