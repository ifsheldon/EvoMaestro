An effective strategy to handle highly non-convex multi-objective optimization paths is to perform "zero-shot multi-start seeding". Here, we provide several explicitly distinct, asymmetric path templates—such as pre-school bumps, broad post-school bends, and heavily inverted paths—evaluate what the exact "iteration zero" loss surface would be for each, and then start the Adam optimizer in the basin that looks the best from the very beginning.

This prevents the optimizer from getting stuck trying to symmetrically balance school and base if an asymmetric path (e.g. going quickly to the side before the school, then dipping towards the base) already yields inherently better tradeoffs in length and noise penalty.

Here is the exact modification implementing a 12-seed zero-shot evaluation multi-start.
