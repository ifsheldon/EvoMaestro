An excellent way to improve both signal proximity (F3) and path travel distance (F1) simultaneously is to introduce **asymmetric local base attraction**. 

In the previous version, the cost function optimization step pulled all coordinates linearly towards the base station's horizontal line `y = by` uniformably. This unnecessarily bends regions of the path far away from the base's `x` position, artificially increasing flight length F1 without conferring signal benefits.

By replacing the global pull with an `x`-coordinate mapped, geometric-aware asymmetric Gaussian envelop (wider before reaching the base, tighter after passing it), the drone creates a broader curve towards the base to circumvent the school organically, and rebounds quicker to the exit point. This cuts unneeded deviations on the way out and sharply improves signal loss locally.
